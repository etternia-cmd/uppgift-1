How does one compete in Rhythm Games?

There are many ways to compete in rhythm games, but the most common way is through online leaderboards, where players can compete for the highest score on a particular song or global leaderboards, meaning that someone could be ranked higher than another person. Every rhythm game its own unique scoring system. Interestingly enough, there arent two scoring systems that are the same across any rhythm game. For example, osu! uses a scoring system based on pp (short for performance points) which is based on a very complicated formula that calculates the amount of notes hit, the distance between them on a grid and their speed (for example speeding up the bpm of a map makes it harder, therefore giving it more performance points). Of course there are other factors such as timing windows, the size of the notes, accuracy, density and muuuch more that I cant fit into this segment.

On the other hand, Etterna uses a scoring system based solely on accuracy, meaning that the more accurate you are, the higher your score will be. The scoring system is based on a percentage of how many notes you hit accurately, with a maximum score of 100%. The more accurate you are, the higher your score will be. There are also other factors such as timing windows, judgements (this isnt a thing in osu for example, judgement refers to the visual indicators for how well you hit a note for example hitting a marvelous, perfect, great, ok, bad etc) that affect your score. It doesnt use performance points it rather rates the maps with a difficulty meter (ranging from 0 to 45 or above) where it then calculates how impressive your score is based on how hard the map that you played is (for example you played a 21 rated map such as in the example video on the other page) and what accuracy you got at the end of it (having a 99% would give you a rating of 24 or 25, while having 90% would give you a rating of around 17 or 18)

What do players compete for?

Every player is different and wants to push and test their limits in different ways. Some players may focus solely on having the highest rank on the global leaderboards, others for having the most amount of leaderboard scores on various types of maps, some focus on playing in specific community made tournaments where they want to show off in front of a live audience and maybe even make some money. What they compete for ultimately depends on their personal goals and motivations, which is what makes the competitive scene so diverse and exciting.You dont have to be one of the best and set records, you can compete against yourself and your friends, improve your own abilities and have fun.

How do you improve at rhythm games?

Speaking of improving your own abilities and having fun, how does one actually get better at rhythm games? Well there are multiple aspects for every rhythm game, but it mainly comes down to understanding the mechanics of the game and playing A LOT. Some of the mechanics include:

  • Reading

    Reading refers to recognizing patterns and anticipating note placements. Most games have different ways of reading but it mainly comes down to how well you can "read" whats coming and knowing how to hit it accordingly.

  • Timing

    Timing refers to hitting notes at the correct timing window. Hitting any note early or late results in dropping accuracy. (if you are hitting notes not in the timing window, are you even playing the game correctly? its like playing a piano without hitting the keys on time, its just not gonna sound right now would it)

  • Consistency

    Consistency refers to maintaining a high level of performance throughout a song or map. This involves avoiding large drops in accuracy and maintaining a steady rhythm, which could come in handy if you are playing a long map (something above 10 minutes for example)

  • Stamina

    Stamina is basically the ability to maintain speed for long periods of time. its a vague concept thats hard to explain as there are different types of stamina, such as tapping stamina (being able to tap notes quickly for extended periods) reading stamina (the ability to maintain focus and accuracy while reading patterns over extended periods) etc.
